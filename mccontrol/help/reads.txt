Facts: what is known about a repeater

What a device has told you is parsed into its settings, each with the age
of the answer that carried it: name and firmware ("get name", "ver"), tx,
rx and direct tx delay ("get txdelay" ...), autoreply and its hops, mode,
channel, private, slots or delay ("get autoreply" gives six at once), the
region map ("region"), and every other "get <setting>" by its name. A
write teaches as much as a read: "set txdelay 0.7" answered OK makes the
tx delay 0.7, as of then.

The newest answer wins. A refusal or silence teaches nothing and clears
nothing, so an old value stays with its age showing how old it is. An
answer whose signature failed (BAD SIGNATURE) teaches nothing at all; an
unsigned one, from firmware older than ar-11, still counts and is marked
unsigned. A read that is not parsed (neighbors, board, memory, the
"stats (Beacon)" line) is listed after the facts, raw, under its command.

Nothing is sent to show them: they come from what is kept on this machine,
the last answer to each read and the history of every command, and they
change the moment a result lands. Any line can be read again. The API has
them as "GET /devices/{ref}/facts"; on the command line, "mccontrol reads
<device>" prints the last answer to each read.
