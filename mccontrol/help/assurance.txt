Can a command be forged, altered or replayed?

The broker is not trusted. Its credentials are shared, so assume anyone
can read every topic and publish to every topic. What stops them is
checked on the repeater, and only there.

What the signature covers
  The repeater verifies an Ed25519 signature over the topic, a newline,
  and "v1|counter|expiry|command". Every byte of that is covered, so a
  changed counter, a longer expiry, a different command, a touched
  signature, or the same payload on another node's topic, another
  region's topic or the broadcast topic all fail with sig-invalid. Only
  the one public key the repeater was given over its serial console
  verifies; the key on this machine never leaves it inside anything but
  a signature.

Why the counter stops a replay
  A repeater stores the counter of every command it accepts, before it
  runs the command, and refuses any counter that is not greater than the
  stored one. The same envelope published again -- a minute later or a
  month later, by you or by anyone -- is refused as replay. The store
  survives a reboot. mccontrol uses unix seconds as the counter, so a
  new command always carries a higher one.

Why the expiry matters as well
  A captured envelope the repeater has not seen yet could be held and
  published later. The expiry limits that to the envelope's life (five
  minutes by default, an hour at most; the repeater refuses anything
  further off than that), and the counter still lets it run once at
  most. The same holds for a broadcast: a repeater that was offline
  when it was sent will accept it once if it is republished within its
  life, which is what you asked for anyway. A repeater with no clock
  refuses rather than guesses.

What this does not give you
  Secrecy: commands and replies are readable by anyone on the broker.
  Nothing secret travels in a command; the settings that would be
  (the signing key, the identity key, erase) are not admitted at all.
  Delivery: the broker can drop or delay a command. That shows as
  silence or as expired, never as a wrong command running.
  Trustworthy results: a result line is not signed. Anyone on the
  broker can publish "ok" in a repeater's name, so a result on its own
  proves only that something answered. Confirm a change with a read
  ("get ...") whose answer you can judge, or by what the repeater then
  does on the air. Signed results need a firmware change; it is on the
  backlog.
  A stolen key: whoever reads key.seed can sign as you. It is kept
  readable only by you; replacing it (Keys menu) silences every
  repeater until each is re-provisioned over serial.

How this is checked
  tests/test_envelope.py builds envelopes and judges them the way the
  firmware does, in the same order: signature, counter, expiry. Each
  property above has a test that goes red if the rule is loosened.
