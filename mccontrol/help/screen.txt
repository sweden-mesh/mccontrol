What an interface shows

Every interface shows the same things, laid out its own way.

The device directory: one row per repeater you manage, with its name,
node id, region, when you last sent to it, its battery and last-seen age
once asked for, its firmware version and what it last answered. One row
is the current device. Rows can be tagged, one at a time, inverted, or a
whole region at once; a verb acts on the tagged devices, or on the
current one when none is tagged. The count of tags is always shown.

The current device's history, newest first (see [[history]]), and what it
last answered to each read, with its age (see [[reads]]). After a send,
each device's result; after a discovery, what answered.

Batt and Seen come from Beacon when you ask; see [[stats]].

A name ending in " ?" marks a device something was sent to and nothing
heard from for seven days -- no result over the control plane and no
status seen by Beacon. It is a device to look at, not a verdict: the
control plane may be off, or the node may answer another key now. A
device never sent to carries no mark, since nothing is known. Nothing
refreshes by itself; statistics, Batt/Seen and names are read when you
ask.

Every wait -- a send, a discovery, a set, a read from Beacon -- shows what
is happening and the running count, and can be stopped early; what has
come is kept.

On a narrow terminal not every column fits: the directory columns are
chosen from Reg, Sent, Batt, Seen, Ver and Result, after the name and
node ("mccontrol config --columns ver,result" does the same).

See [[keys]] for the keys, [[send]] for sending, [[discover]] for finding
repeaters.
